I have written a lot of software that no longer exists. Companies folded, products were retired, servers were quietly switched off. None of it was wasted, but very little of it _stayed_.

What stays is stranger and smaller. A way of thinking a colleague handed me in a single sentence. The shape of a problem I finally understood at two in the morning. A line in a book I read at sixteen that I am still arguing with.

## Software forgets, people remember

We talk about systems as if they were permanent. They are not. The most robust architecture I ever built is gone, and the most fragile friendships from that period are still here.

> The work is the excuse. The people and the ideas are the point.

When I started programming at twelve, I thought the goal was to build something that would last forever. I no longer believe in forever. I believe in things that are worth the attention while they are here, and worth remembering once they are not.

## Why I write this down

This site is a small attempt to keep the things that stay in one place — the essays, the books, the music, the conversations. Not as a monument, but as a notebook left open on the desk.

If something here stays with you long after you close the tab, then it did its job.
