I came to wine the way I came to most things: through the structure. Regions, balance, the way a good bottle ages — the slow chemistry of something getting better while you do nothing but wait.

It is the most patient hobby I have. You cannot rush it. You cannot ship a hotfix to a 2009 Barolo.

## The disciplines that take years

The things I value most all share this shape. They reward attention and refuse to be hurried:

1. Writing well, which is mostly rewriting.
2. Building a company, which is mostly not quitting.
3. Raising anything — a child, a vineyard, a culture — which is mostly showing up.

Software trained me to want fast feedback loops. Wine taught me that some loops are measured in decades, and that this is a feature, not a bug.

## On not optimising everything

There is a kind of person who tries to optimise their wine the way they optimise their inbox. I understand the impulse. I have it too. But some things are diminished by efficiency.

> Patience is not the absence of action. It is the willingness to let the right thing take the time it actually takes.

I am still not very good at it. But I am, slowly, getting better — which is, I suppose, the whole point.
