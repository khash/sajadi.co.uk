title: On Building, and Letting Go

I sold my first software to a local business as a teenager in Tehran. It solved a real problem for them, badly, and they paid me for it anyway. I have been chasing that feeling ever since — the moment something you made in your bedroom starts living a life of its own out in the world.

What nobody tells you is that the building is the easy half. The hard half is letting go.

## Everything you build leaves

Every product I have shipped has eventually stopped being mine. Users bend it into shapes I never intended. Co-founders take it in directions I would not have chosen. Time and the market do the rest.

For a long time this felt like loss. Now it feels like the point. A thing that only you can hold is a hobby. A thing that can survive without you is a business — and, occasionally, a gift.

## The teenager was right

The kid selling clunky software in Tehran understood something I keep having to relearn: you build the thing, you hand it over, and then you go build the next thing. The handing-over is not the failure. It is the whole arc.

Build it well. Let it go cleanly. Start again.
